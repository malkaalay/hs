Artist Story

From Survival To Music

I’m Loai Miqdad, known as Hicats Sam / HS. I’m from Gaza City, Palestine, and I use music and visual storytelling to speak from my own experience.

Before music became the main direction I wanted to follow, my background was in software and database development. I studied the field and worked as a developer, but music had always been something I loved.

Before the war, most of that passion was connected to Arabic music. I never expected that I would build a large part of my artistic identity through English rap. The turning point came during our first displacement, when I saw MC Abdul, a young rapper from Gaza, performing in English and speaking about Gaza.

I remember thinking: I am living this story too. Why shouldn’t I be one of the people telling it myself?

That is when English started becoming a bridge for me. I wanted people who could not understand Arabic to still hear directly from someone living through the war, displacement, fear and loss of home in Gaza.

I started listening more seriously to NF, Eminem and Linkin Park. NF influenced me through storytelling and emotion. Eminem pushed me to study flow, rhyme, delivery and wordplay. Linkin Park showed me how pain, anger, vulnerability and energy can exist inside the same piece of music.

I also taught myself English and performance through anything I could find: YouTube, podcasts, interviews, vlogs and music. I would repeat words, record myself, listen back and try again.

The journey was never completely individual. Billy Salisbury from the UK has stood beside me through a lot of it, helping me with pronunciation, accent, flow, delivery, singing, writing and confidence. His support has been continuous, not just one lesson or one song.

Garry Mc from The Kabin Studio in Ireland also helped me understand rap more practically. He wrote 16 bars for me as an exercise and stayed with me step by step until I could perform the full piece, working with me on rhythm, rhyme, entering the beat and finding the right flow.

In Gaza, Qism was my friend and producer. He gave me access to his studio, helped me record and produce, and gave me his time without asking me for money. More than that, he believed in my music and in where it could take me.

My family lost our home during the war. We went through repeated displacement and periods of living in tents, with unstable electricity, unstable internet and very limited equipment. Things that are basic for many artists can become part of the production problem here.

FEAR was the project where I learned how far I could go with what I had.

FEAR is my cover and reinterpretation of NF’s FEAR. I wanted to be clear about that from the beginning because I respect the original work. I connected with the song emotionally and built my own visual interpretation around fear, memory, pressure, isolation and what I was living through in Gaza.

The project took around eight months to complete. The entire video was filmed on an iPhone 16 Pro Max with friends who believed in the vision. I did not have my own laptop to edit on, so I used a friend’s laptop after he finished his own work. Because I could not afford professional post-production, I taught myself editing while making the project: cutting scenes, working on color, pacing, reshooting and learning new techniques as I went.

I lost Qism during the war only a few months before FEAR was officially released on 19 July 2026. I lost a close friend, but I also lost the person who had made music production possible for me when I could not afford normal studio costs.

That is one of the biggest challenges now. I have ideas, lyrics and projects I want to make, but producing them became much harder after losing Qism.

I do not want to be seen only as “an artist from Gaza.” Gaza is a huge part of who I am, but I also want people to see the artist, the writing, the performance, the visual ideas and the ambition. I do not claim to speak for everyone in Gaza. I speak from my own experience.

The next step is my original project AFTER FEAR, currently built around four songs: Homeless, Lost Mom, Bad Story and Can You Hold Me, the last of which includes a collaboration with Rita Mousi.

I am also working on a cover of Linkin Park’s In the End with Raji Al Jaru and Rita Mousi. We are changing parts of the approach so the performance connects more closely with our reality in Gaza instead of simply copying the original song.

For me, FEAR was not the end. It was the beginning of the artist I am still trying to become.

Transparency: FEAR is based primarily on real-location footage, real performers and my own direction, filming and editing. Three very brief symbolic sequences use AI-generated imagery: the writing appearing on the wall, the door sequence and the piano sequence. They were used as artistic metaphors, not to recreate events or present fabricated documentary material.
